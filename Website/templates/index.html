      How the model works — and what it cannot do

      Two separate answers

      A MobileNetV2 network pre-trained on ImageNet is fine-tuned on 16 classes built from eight fruits × two conditions. Fruit identity is the summed probability of that fruit's fresh and rotten classes; the condition is the eight fresh probabilities against the eight rotten ones.

      The two are reported separately because they can differ in confidence. A photograph can show a pomegranate beyond doubt while leaving fresh versus rotten genuinely ambiguous — in that case the site names the fruit and marks the condition uncertain, instead of discarding a correct identification.

      What it cannot do

      • Shelf life. No capture dates, storage temperatures or days-to-spoilage labels exist in the dataset, so no number of days can be predicted.
      • Cross-sections. Only a small, unlabelled fraction of the dataset shows cut fruit, so cut-fruit analysis is not validated.
      • Food safety. A photograph cannot prove a fruit is safe to eat.
      • Other fruits. Fruits outside the eight supported types may be forced into a supported class. The gate reports low confidence when that happens, but it is not a guarantee.

      How uncertainty is handled

      Acceptance thresholds are fitted on the validation split and stored with the model. Capture-quality checks — blur, exposure, size — only produce advice; they never override a fruit the network recognises with high confidence.
